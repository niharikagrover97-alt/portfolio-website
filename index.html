what I bring

Why me

Research is where I do my best thinking — but I bring more than one lens to the table.

no. 1

Research rigor, not just research vibes

I design studies around real decisions: clear research questions, the right method for the job, and findings traceable to evidence — qualitative or quantitative.

no. 2

I speak fluent designer

With a design practice of my own, I can prototype what I test and test what I design. Insights land faster when the researcher can sketch the implication.

no. 3

An artist’s trained eye

Fine art taught me to observe before interpreting — to notice what’s actually there, not what I expect to see. It’s the same muscle great research is built on.

no. 4

Insights people actually use

A finding only matters if it changes a decision. I synthesize with teams rather than at them, and craft readouts that are impossible to ignore.

methods & craft

How I work

01 — discover

Generative research

In-depth interviews, diary studies, contextual inquiry, and field observation to understand needs before solutions exist.

02 — evaluate

Evaluative research

Moderated and unmoderated usability testing, heuristic evaluation, concept testing, and benchmarking studies.

03 — measure

Quantitative methods

Surveys, card sorting, tree testing, SUS/UMUX scoring, and behavioral analytics to size and validate qualitative findings.

04 — synthesize

Insight synthesis

Affinity mapping, journey mapping, and persona development that turn raw data into shared team understanding.

05 — design

Design & prototyping

Wireframes, flows, and interactive prototypes in Figma — to explore solutions and to build the stimuli my studies need.

06 — communicate

Storytelling & advocacy

Research readouts, highlight reels, and insight repositories that make findings travel across teams.

I’m Niharika — a UX researcher and designer who started out as a fine artist, which means I was trained to observe long before I learned to interview.

That habit of really looking — at people, at behavior, at what’s unsaid — is what pulled me into research. Today I pair it with design skills that let me move from insight to prototype without losing anything in translation.
